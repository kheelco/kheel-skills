# Concepts

Each concept with a plain explanation, an example, and where the rule for it lives. Examples use a club's membership
roll and a fleet of vehicles; use the person's own domain instead where one is to hand.

## The core (Ulwick's Outcome-Driven Innovation)

**Job to be done.** What someone is trying to get done, described independently of how they do it today. People do
not want a membership database; they want *an accurate, current record of who belongs to the club*. The job stays the
same while the tools come and go, which is why it is a stable thing to understand and to design for.

**Functional job.** The practical accomplishment at the centre of the analysis, stated as **verb + object + contextual
clarifier**: *Maintain* *the record of who belongs to the club and on what terms*, *over the life of the club*. It
contains no solution, technology or process, and is kept concise: how well the job must be done (*accurate and
current*) and what its object covers are recorded beside it, as its success criteria and scope. (`job-topology`, *The core functional job*.)

**Job executor.** The person or organisation trying to get the job done, defined by the **responsibility** they carry,
not their title: *the person or organisation responsible for the club's record of its members*. A volunteer secretary,
a committee member and a paid administrator can all be the same executor. (`job-topology`, *The executor*.)

**Related jobs.** Other jobs the same executor is trying to get done around the main one, before, during or after it,
each for a different result (*provide information about membership*). (`job-topology`, *The other jobs*.)

**Emotional and social jobs.** How the executor wants to feel (*avoid the anxiety of being blamed when a member is
wrongly turned away*) and be seen (*be seen as a dependable keeper of the club's records*) while doing the job.
Recorded as qualifiers on the job, and later turned into outcomes. (`job-topology`, *Qualifiers and circumstances*.)

**Job map.** An inventory of what the executor must accomplish to get the job done, organised by eight universal
**stages**: Define, Locate, Prepare, Confirm, Execute, Monitor, Modify, Conclude. A step sits under a stage for what it
contributes, not for when it happens, so the steps need not form one sequence, and one occasion of the job may use only
some of them. The stages are questions to ask about any job (what has to be
worked out first? gathered? checked?), not boxes to fill. A typical map has ten to twenty steps. It is not a process
map: it says what must be accomplished, not how. (`job-map`.)

**Job step.** One thing the executor must accomplish, stated as a milestone: *Confirm a change is authorised and the
person entitled to it*, not *run the approval check*. (`job-map`, *Step rules*.)

**Desired outcome.** How the executor judges a step done well, in a fixed form: **direction + measure + object +
clarifier**. *Minimise the likelihood that a change is entered without the authority it requires.* *Minimise the time
between a change and its evidence reaching the secretary.* Outcomes are the executor's own standards, measurable, and
free of solutions; they are what products are later judged against. (`job-map`, *outcomes*.)

**Opportunity scoring.** Surveying executors on how important each outcome is and how well it is met today, to find
the important, underserved ones. It is research built on a finished map; the map's outcomes are written ready for it.
(`job-map`, *The opportunity survey*.)

## Ideas the plugin adds

These are this plugin's own, consistent with Ulwick but not part of his method ([extensions](extensions.md)).

**Job topology.** A compact inventory of the domain's jobs and executors, made before any job is mapped, so the map is
pointed at the right job. Ulwick does not prescribe one.

**Stakeholder jobs.** Jobs of other executors that touch the main job: *decide on an application* (the committee),
*join the club* (the applicant). Ulwick gives them no single name; the topology lists those that help establish the
main job's boundary. (`job-topology`, *The other jobs*.)

**Constituent candidates.** Work the topology finds that seems to make up the main job (taking in changes, checking,
correcting, setting up), recorded with what was noticed and a provisional lean, but not yet classified. The job map
classifies each one as it maps the job (covered by its steps, worth a map of its own, outside the job, or unresolved)
and reports back.

**Job, episode or step?** Five tests are weighed: an independent objective, an independent completion, the executor,
whether it makes sense outside another job's work, and whether it would survive a change of tools. No single test
decides; having its own trigger and end does not make something a job (steps have both), and a different success
criterion (checking for consistency, or for correctness) is an outcome, not a job. The topology uses the tests lightly,
at its boundary and to form a lean; the job map applies them in full.

**A different job, or not?** A different functional objective is a different job. A different motivation for the
same job (buying to live in or to let), a different circumstance (an auction), or a higher-order job the focal job is
one means to (*secure a place to live*) is not a restatement of it. Ask what the executor is trying to get done, not
why. (`job-topology`, *A different job, or not?*.)

**Open classification.** A question at the edge of the main job that the tests genuinely split on (is this part of the
main job, or a job beside it?), recorded with the evidence each way instead of being forced.

**Continuing job and episodes.** Some jobs never finish: they keep something in a state (*maintain the roll*, *keep
the fleet roadworthy*). They are done through **episodes**, each set off by something: a change arrives, a check falls
due, an error is found. In a job map, each episode uses the steps it needs.

**Sub-job.** A constituent the job map finds worth mapping on its own, because it has a distinct functional objective:
*service a vehicle* within *keep the fleet roadworthy*. Its work can be covered by the larger job's map and still be
mapped on its own at a finer level; having its own sequence is not enough. It is added to the topology and mapped
alongside the larger job's map. Separately mappable parts do not, by their number, make the larger job the wrong unit: that is judged by its
objective. (`job-map`.)

**Circumstances and edge cases.** A **circumstance** is a situation the whole job is done in (a first set-up, a large
organisation, a dispute); an **edge case** is a particular kind of thing the job deals with (a family membership, a
joint holding). Both are written on the steps they change: *Junior member: confirm a parent or guardian has consented.*
(`job-map`, *Circumstances and edge cases*.)

**Seam.** Where the work leaves any system and happens in the world (a signature, a payment, a court order), and
whether its result just arrives or the act of recording makes it real. Keeps a map honest about what software could
ever do.

**Glossary.** Every job, step and outcome is written in the domain's own words, held in the glossary, so everyone
reads them the same way. (`glossary`.)

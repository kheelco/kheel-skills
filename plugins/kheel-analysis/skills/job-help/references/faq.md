# Common questions

**Is this a process map?** No. A job map is an inventory of what the executor must accomplish, organised by stage,
not how, in what order or in what system. Its steps need not form one sequence, and one occasion of the job may use
only some of them. If a map only makes sense with a screen in mind, or reads as the handling of one case from start
to finish, it has become a workflow.

**Is this Ulwick?** The topology and job map together are Ulwick-aligned: an ODI practitioner will recognise the
functional job, executor, eight-stage map and outcomes. The topology artefact itself, stakeholder jobs, constituent
candidates, job domain analysis, sub-jobs, episodes, variants and seams, are this plugin's extension
([extensions](extensions.md)).

**Related job or stakeholder job?** A related job is another job the *same* executor is trying to get done, which is
Ulwick's term and his meaning. A stakeholder job is a job of *another* executor that touches the main job; the word
is this plugin's, since Ulwick gives such jobs no single name.

**Why not start with the map?** Because a map of the wrong job, or one stated at the wrong altitude, is wasted work.
The topology is usually short; it is there to make sure the map is pointed at the right job.

**Do I need job domain analysis?** Usually not. Use it when the boundaries between jobs matter to what you are doing
([extensions](extensions.md), *When the extension is worth it*).

**What's the difference between a job and a step?** A job has its own result that the executor sets out to achieve and
judges; a step only makes sense inside a job, on the way to its result. *Obtain the committee's approval* is a step:
nobody does it for its own sake.

**My job never finishes. How do I map it?** Map it over its life: one map holding the steps every kind of work needs
(a change arriving, a periodic check, a correction), each using the steps it needs. If some of that work has a
distinct objective worth mapping on its own, it can also be mapped as a sub-job, at a finer level, while staying
covered by the main map. The topology notes such work as constituent candidates; the map classifies it.

**Someone else does the work for them. Is it still their job?** Yes. An executor who engages a lawyer, agent or
registrar still pursues the job, and the work done for them is still its steps. Whose objective is achieved decides,
not who performs the action; the professional's own job may be recorded as a stakeholder job.

**Why does the executor's title not matter?** Because the same job is done by people with different titles, and people
with the same title do different jobs. The executor is defined by the responsibility they carry.

**Where do circumstances and edge cases go?** On the steps they change, as *case: what the step must accomplish then*.
A situation of the whole job (a first set-up, a dispute) is a circumstance; a particular kind of thing (a family
membership, a joint holding) is an edge case. Neither gets a map of its own.

**How many steps and outcomes should there be?** Typically ten to twenty steps across the eight stages, and two to four
outcomes per significant step. Fewer usually means things are stated too broadly; many more, that the job is stated
too high.

**What does "evidence: inferred" mean?** While drafting, everything is marked attested (a source said so), inferred
(concluded from evidence) or proposed (a modelling choice). It keeps guesses from passing as facts. The agreed
documents drop the marks, and keep any real doubt as an open question.

**Can I change things later?** Yes. Each skill says how a change ripples (renaming a job, splitting a step), and the
documents are written to be revised as understanding improves.

# The modelling process, stage by stage

A recommended path, not a gate: let the person skip, reorder or stop. At each stage, say what it produces and why it
comes now, ask the one or two questions that start it (or hand over to the skill that does it), and check the stage's
*ready to move on* test before offering the next.

## 0. Set up

**Ask:** What domain is this, and what is the analysis for (understanding the domain, ODI research, shaping a product
later)? What do you have already: notes, interview transcripts, documents, a glossary, an earlier analysis? Where
should the documents live?

**Produces:** a folder for the domain, and an agreed purpose. **Ready to move on:** the domain and purpose are clear
enough to say who might be trying to get what done.

**Tip:** if there are notes or transcripts, start by harvesting them; asking questions the material already answers
wastes the practitioner's time.

## 1. The words (glossary)

**Why now:** every later artefact is written in the domain's terms; a job statement with an undefined noun is
ambiguous.

**Ask:** What are the things this domain talks about? Which words do people argue about, or use differently?
**Skill:** `glossary`.

**Ready to move on:** the nouns the main job will be written in are defined, at least provisionally. The glossary
grows throughout; it does not need to be finished.

## 2. The jobs (job topology)

**Why now:** a job map of the wrong job is worthless, however good.

**Ask:** What is someone here ultimately trying to get done, true under any process? Who is responsible for it? What
else do they, and others, do around it? **Skill:** `job-topology`.

**Ready to move on:** the main job, its executor and its principal boundaries are clear enough to map; work that seems
to make up the main job is recorded as constituent candidates (what was noticed, and a lean), without deciding what
each is; boundary questions that cannot be settled are recorded as open. A small topology is fine.

## 3. Optional: how the jobs relate (job domain analysis)

**Why now, or not at all:** only when a boundary question in the topology matters to the map, or several executors'
jobs depend on one another in ways the topology's short descriptions do not capture ([extensions](extensions.md),
*When the extension is worth it*). It can also come later, when a map exposes a problem at the edge of a job. Whether
parts of the main job are jobs of their own is not a reason for it: the map works that out. Its own ground is seams,
obligations and boundary tests; identity between jobs is the topology's boundary work done more thoroughly.

**Ask:** Which boundary question matters most? How do these jobs depend on each other? **Skill:**
`job-domain-analysis`.

**Ready to move on:** the boundary questions the map depends on are settled or left open with evidence; any changes to
the topology are proposed and agreed.

## 4. The job map

**Why now:** this is where the job becomes concrete: what must be accomplished, and how the executor judges it.

**Ask:** Confirm the job and executor. Then work the stages as questions: what has to be worked out first? Gathered?
Made ready? Checked? Done? Watched? Put right? Finished? Where does it happen outside any system? What situations and
particular cases change a step? As the map takes shape, resolve each of the topology's constituent candidates:
absorbed (an episode or a step), confirmed as a sub-job if it has a progression and outcomes of its own, or deferred
with a reason. Report
each resolution back to the topology; if several sub-jobs appear, ask whether the main job is really a higher-order
objective. **Skill:** `job-map`.

**Ready to move on:** every stage has been asked about, the steps found are stated as milestones, circumstances and
edge cases are on the steps they change, every constituent candidate is resolved (or deferred, with why) and reported to
the topology, and the
questions that could change the job's boundary are recorded. (If a sub-job was confirmed, the topology now lists it;
map it in turn if it matters to the purpose.)

## 5. Desired outcomes

**Why now:** outcomes need the steps to attach to.

**Ask:** For each step, how do they know it went well? What takes too long, goes wrong, or varies from person to
person? **Skill:** `job-map` (*outcomes*).

**Ready to move on:** each significant step has two to four outcomes in the standard form, each checked as the
executor's own standard, measurable and free of solutions.

## 6. Test it

**Why now:** a model that has never met a real case is a hypothesis.

**Ask:** Tell me about a recent real case, an awkward one if possible. Can it be traced through the map, step by step,
in the glossary's words? Where it cannot, something is missing or wrong. (A *scenario* skill is planned for this; until
then, trace cases by hand and note what breaks.)

**Ready to move on:** the cases tried pass through the map, or what they broke has been fixed or recorded.

## 7. Next

Offer, depending on the purpose: an opportunity survey on the outcomes (ODI research); mapping another job from the topology (a
related or stakeholder job, or a confirmed sub-job); a review of any artefact (each skill has one); or stopping here.

## Guiding well

- **One stage at a time**, and one or two questions at a time within it.
- **Say where they are** at each step: *we have the jobs; next is the map of the main one*.
- **Keep the purpose in view**: for ODI research, go straight from topology to map to outcomes; bring in domain analysis
  only when a boundary question would change the map.
- **Record, don't invent**: an unanswered question is noted as open, never filled with a plausible answer.
- **Hand over cleanly**: name the skill doing the work, and come back to the guide when it is done.

# Elicitation

## Asking a practitioner

One or two questions at a time.

1. **Confirm the job and executor.** Read back the job statement and the executor's capacity, and ask whether that is
   what they are trying to get done.
2. **Work the step types as questions**, in order (see [steps](steps.md)): what do they have to work out first, gather,
   set up, check, do, watch, fix, and finish? Write down only what is found.
3. **For each step, ask what accomplishing it means**: the milestone, not the task. *What do you have once that is
   done?* Rewrite until the verb is the executor's and no mechanism remains.
4. **Mark the seams.** For each step: does this happen in any system, or arrive from the world?
5. **For each step, ask how they know it went well.** Then: what takes too long, what goes wrong, what varies from
   person to person or case to case? Each answer is an outcome waiting to be stated.
6. **Read the map back as a story** and ask whether anything is missing or out of place.

Record each answer's source so steps and outcomes can be marked attested.

## Harvesting from notes

Harvest first, then ask about the gaps:

- every **"then we have to…"** and **"before we can…"**: a step;
- every **"the hard part is…"**: a step, often an underserved one;
- every **"it always goes wrong when…"**, **"it takes forever to…"**, **"no two people do it the same way"**: an
  outcome (likelihood, time, variability);
- every **"I need to know that…"** or **"we have to be sure…"**: a Confirm or Monitor step, or an outcome on one;
- every **other party** who does part of the work: a related job, not a step.

Restate each as verb + glossary noun (a step) or in the outcome form (an outcome) before placing it. Everything
harvested is at best inferred until a source states it.

# Job identity, decomposition and episodes

The examples below are drawn from several domains (a club's membership roll, a fleet of vehicles, a clinic) to
illustrate rules that hold in any domain. None of them is a fact about the domain being analysed.

## Four distinct ideas

The topology may leave candidates whose classification is open: is this a job of its own, part of a larger job, an
occasion on which a job is done, or something another job depends on? Four ideas answer it, and they are not mutually
exclusive:

| Idea | What it says | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Job identity** | This is an independently meaningful accomplishment: a functional job, worth mapping on its own | *Service a vehicle* |
| **Decomposition** | This is a smaller undertaking that contributes to a larger one | *Service a vehicle* is part of *keep the fleet roadworthy* |
| **Episode** | This is an occurrence of a job: a time or setting in which it is done, with its trigger | *The annual service falls due* |
| **Relationship** | Two distinct jobs depend on or act on each other | *Report a fault* (driver) *changes* what *keep the fleet roadworthy* must deal with |

A **sub-job** has job identity *and* is part of a larger job: being part of something larger does not stop it being a
functional job in its own right. Decomposition and job identity are separate questions, and both are answered from the
evidence, not assumed.

## Job identity: is it a functional job of its own?

A shared higher-level objective is **evidence of relatedness, not of identity**. Inspecting, servicing and repairing
vehicles all serve a roadworthy fleet; that alone neither makes them one job nor separates them. Weigh several tests
together:

1. **A stable executor objective.** Is there a result the executor sets out to achieve, which they would recognise and
   name, and judge done well or badly (*the vehicle is serviced*)? An activity whose only result is a step towards
   something else (*obtain the parts*) is a step.
2. **A coherent accomplishment.** Does it have its own beginning, execution and completion, with a completion condition
   of its own? Activities that only make sense inside another's execution are steps of it.
3. **Independence.** Could it be done, and judged, without the others happening in the same sitting? A check that can
   be run when nothing has changed is independent of taking in changes.
4. **Stability.** Would it still be a distinct accomplishment under a different process or technology?
5. **The stage test, as support only.** That it could run Define to Conclude on its own supports identity; almost any
   complex activity can, so it never decides alone.

What never decides identity on its own: the same executor; the same higher-level objective; a different trigger,
frequency or size; a different source of instruction or authority (a fault found by the mechanic and one reported in a
recall lead to the same repair); a different organisational setting.

Conclude for each candidate: **a functional job** (with or without a place in a decomposition), **a step** of a named
job, **an episode** of a named job, or **undecided** (recorded as open, with the evidence each way).

## Decomposition: what is part of what

A job may be decomposed into **sub-jobs** where its parts have job identity and together make it up: *maintain the
membership roll* into *record a change to the roll*, *check the roll against its sources* and *correct the roll*.

- **Each sub-job keeps its identity**: its own statement, executor objective and completion. It can be mapped on its
  own, and the higher job can still be mapped over its life; which to map is the job map's choice.
- **Sub-jobs share the higher job's executor.** Work done by another executor is a related job, not a sub-job.
- **Decomposition is not required.** A continuing job with no parts of independent identity has episodes, not sub-jobs.
- **Several levels are allowed**, but more than two is usually a sign that the higher job is stated above the level of a
  functional job (*run the club*).

## Continuing jobs and their episodes

Some jobs have a beginning, execution and end (*settle an estate*). Others are **continuing**: they keep something in a
desired state indefinitely (*keep the fleet roadworthy*, *maintain the membership roll*). A continuing job shows in the
work its executor does when nothing new arrives: a fault found on inspection, a discrepancy with old records, a
dispute, a rebuild after loss, a question about the state at a past date.

A continuing job is done through **episodes**, each with a trigger:

| Trigger | Example episode (membership roll) | Example episode (fleet) |
|---|---|---|
| **Event-driven**: something arrives | A change to the roll | A reported fault |
| **Periodic**: a cycle comes round | A check against the roll's sources | A scheduled service |
| **Exceptional**: something is found wrong | A correction | A vehicle withdrawn from use |

Each episode is then classified by the identity tests above: an episode may simply be an occasion of the job, or the
occasion of a sub-job (the scheduled service is an episode of *service a vehicle*, which is a sub-job of *keep the fleet
roadworthy*). Record, for each episode, its trigger, its **immediate result** (what that piece of work leaves done) and
its **completion** (how the executor knows it is finished); these are the evidence for the classification.

- **Keep episodes at one altitude.** An occasional undertaking far larger than the rest (rebuilding the state after a
  loss, moving it to a new keeper) is usually a sub-job, not an episode beside the routine ones.
- **The same activity, started two ways.** Putting right a mistake made while work is being done is part of that work;
  putting right one found long afterwards, with evidence to reconstruct and authority to establish, may have identity
  of its own.

## Boundary validation

Test the decomposition and the relationships against cases that would break them:

- **Exceptional cases**: the rare, large or contested occurrence (a recovery, a dispute, a first set-up);
- **Different settings**: a small and a large organisation; an organisation where the executor's role is formally
  appointed and one where it is not;
- **Change**: a different technology or regulation. A boundary that only holds under today's process is a mechanism.

Record what each test showed. A boundary that fails one is reconsidered, not patched.

## Errors to watch for

- **Identity from a shared objective.** Activities merged because they serve the same higher result, or split because
  their triggers differ, without the identity tests.
- **Hierarchy taken for non-identity.** A sub-job denied a map of its own because it is part of something larger.
- **Decomposition by episode.** Every trigger made a sub-job, though the work has no identity of its own.
- **Another executor's work as a sub-job.** It is a related job.
- **Episodes at mixed altitudes.** A rebuild listed beside routine changes.
- **Decided by mapping convenience.** A classification chosen because it gives a tidier map.

# Identity between jobs, and boundary validation

The examples below are drawn from several domains (a club's membership roll, a fleet of vehicles, a clinic) to
illustrate rules that hold in any domain. None of them is a fact about the domain being analysed.

## What this covers, and what it does not

This analysis works **between** jobs: whether two candidates are one job or two, whether a candidate belongs to the
main job or beside it, whether two people are one executor, and whether the boundaries the topology drew hold up.

What happens **inside** a job (whether a piece of work is a step, an episode, or a sub-job with its own map) is
discovered by the job map, which resolves the topology's constituent candidates with the job's progression in view
(`job-map`, *Resolving constituents*). This analysis does not repeat that work; where it needs to know a job's internal
structure, it reads the map.

## Identity between jobs

Questions this answers, usually open classifications from the topology:

- **One job or two?** Two candidates that look alike (*inspect a vehicle* and *service a vehicle*), or one candidate
  that may be two (*provide information about membership*: answering a routine request and resolving a disputed one).
- **Part of the main job, or beside it?** A candidate the topology could not place: within the main job (a
  constituent, left for the map) or a job of its own beside it (related, if the same executor pursues a different
  result; stakeholder, if another executor does).
- **One executor or two?** People who stand to the main job the same way but may pursue different results (a company
  paying a dividend and a lender taking security both rely on the register).

Weigh the topology's **five tests** (`job-topology`, *The five tests*): independent objective, independent completion,
executor, context independence and stable identity, gathering evidence for each. No single test decides.

What never decides on its own: the same executor; the same higher-level objective (evidence of relatedness, not of
identity); a different trigger, frequency or size; a different source of instruction or authority (a fault found by
the mechanic and one reported in a recall lead to the same repair); a different success criterion (which is an outcome,
not a job); a different organisational setting.

Conclude for each question, with the evidence, and **propose the change to the topology**: two jobs where it had one,
one where it had two, a candidate moved into or out of the main job, an executor split or merged. Where the evidence
does not settle it, leave it open with the evidence each way.

## Boundary validation

Test the topology's boundaries, and the relationships between jobs, against cases that would break them:

- **Exceptional cases**: the rare, large or contested occurrence (a recovery, a dispute, a first set-up);
- **Different settings**: a small and a large organisation; one where the executor's role is formally appointed and one
  where it is not;
- **Change**: a different technology or regulation. A boundary that only holds under today's process is a mechanism.

Record what each test showed. A boundary that fails one is reconsidered, not patched.

## Errors to watch for

- **Identity from a shared objective.** Jobs merged because they serve the same higher result, or split because their
  triggers differ, without the five tests.
- **Executors merged by relationship.** Different executors folded into one because they stand to a job the same way.
- **A job per criterion.** Jobs separated by success criteria that are really outcomes of the same work.
- **Internal structure decided here.** Steps, episodes or sub-jobs of a job classified without its map; that is the job
  map's work.
- **Decided by convenience.** A classification chosen because it gives a tidier model or map.

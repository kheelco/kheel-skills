# Executors and jobs

The examples below are drawn from several domains (a club's membership roll, a fleet of vehicles, an estate, a
conference, a clinic) to illustrate rules that hold in any domain. None of them is a fact about the domain being
analysed.

## The executor

Define the executor by their **capacity**: the responsibility they carry in getting the job done (*the person or
organisation responsible for the club's record of its members*), not a title, office, department, seniority or
persona. Give the executor a convenient **name** (*Membership secretary*), an agent term in the glossary, but do not let
the name assume a formal office the domain does not always have: many clubs appoint no membership secretary, and the
job is still done. Where no existing word fits and a name is coined for the capacity, its glossary entry says it names
a functional role, not a formal or statutory office, so readers do not take it for one.

- **Unify when the desired outcome is the same.** People getting the same job done, trying to achieve the same result,
  are one executor, however different their scale, volume, infrastructure, frequency, seniority or obligations. A
  volunteer secretary, a committee member doing it themselves and a paid administrator are one executor; so, at first,
  is a national federation's membership office, whose differences (volume, online joining, regulation) are
  **circumstances** of the same job.
- **Split only when the desired outcome changes.** Where the intent or the shape of the job is materially different, it
  is a different executor, and usually a different job. A treasurer forecasting next year's membership income is not
  the membership secretary at a higher level: the intent (*what if* against *what is*) and the lifecycle (throwaway
  against permanent) differ. More steps, more volume or different infrastructure are not reasons to split.

Record, for each executor, a line on what was unified or split and why.

## Roles are not jobs

An executor's role often spans several jobs, and coordinating someone else's job does not make it theirs. A practice
manager may chase a supplier, book the courier and sign for the delivery; that does not put the supplier's delivery in
the practice manager's job. Ask, of each thing the executor does: **is this getting their job done, or helping someone
else get theirs done?** Help given to another job is, at most, a step of the executor's own job (*confirm the delivery
has been authorised*) or a sign of a stakeholder job.

## The core functional job

State it as **verb + object + contextual clarifier**: *Maintain* *the record of who belongs to the club and on what
terms* *over the life of the club*. The verb and object, without the clarifier, are the job's **name**, which
identifies it (*Maintain the membership roll*).

**Keep the statement concise; record the rest beside it.** The statement is the job's identity, and should stay true
as understanding of the job grows. What discovery finds out about the job goes in fields of its own on the job's
entry, not into the statement:

- **Scope**: what the object takes in and what it leaves out (members of every grade, past and present; not guests),
  with the edge cases that settled it;
- **Success criteria**: how the executor judges the job as a whole done well, in a phrase or two (*accurate and
  current*), with the **standard** for an assurance job (below). These are not desired outcomes: the job map writes
  those, step by step, in the outcome form.

Quality words (*accurate*, *timely*, *complete*, *efficiently*) are success criteria, not part of the job: two
executors who want different qualities are still doing the same job. A clarifier earns its place only where it changes
what the job is (*over the life of the club*, against a single transaction), not where it describes how well it is
done.

It must be free of any solution, independent of current technology, stable enough to outlive any process, stated from
the executor's perspective, and about something they are trying to accomplish.

**One job, one verb.** A statement joined with *and* (*maintain the roll and report to the federation*) is usually two
jobs, or a job and one of its steps: split it and test each part. A compound object is fine where it is one thing the
executor keeps coherent (*who belongs and on what terms*).

It must be **neutral about sequence**: it should not assume whether a change in the world comes before, during or as a
consequence of the job, where that differs between circumstances. *Bring the record into line with a change* assumes
the change has already happened, which is false wherever making the entry is what gives the change effect; *record a
change* holds either way. The same applies to every job in the topology and every job map's heading.

A job whose result is **assurance** (to verify, check, validate, audit or reconcile something) must say which
**standard** it is after:

- **consistency**: the thing agrees with the evidence available for it (a roll that matches the payments received);
- **correctness**: the underlying state is as it should be (everyone on the roll is in fact entitled to be).

Agreement with sources does not establish correctness: sources can be missing, wrong, in conflict, or copied from the
record they are checked against. The two are different results, and may be different jobs. Name the standard in the
job's success criteria (or in the statement, where the standard is what makes it a distinct job), and let the map
follow it.

### Altitude and breadth

- **Would it still be the job under a completely different process?** If not, it is too low.
- **Does it have a beginning and an end, or a standing state with recurring work?** If neither (*run the club*), it is
  too high. If a standing state, see *Continuing jobs* below.
- **Is there one executor for it?** Two executors in one statement is usually two jobs.
- **Is the object the right breadth?** Test it against edge cases from the domain, cases at the edge of what the
  executor must keep coherent (for a membership roll: a family membership, an honorary member, a suspended member, a
  member moving between grades). If the executor cannot handle them without artificial distinctions, the object is too
  narrow (*who belongs* where *who belongs and on what terms* is meant). If widening it brings in several independent
  results, it is too broad. Record the cases tried and what they showed in the job's **scope**, not its statement;
those the object takes in become edge cases for the job map.

### Continuing jobs

Some jobs have a beginning, execution and end (*settle an estate*, *run a conference*). Others are **continuing**: they
keep something in a desired state indefinitely (*keep the fleet roadworthy*, *maintain the membership roll*). A
continuing job is legitimate, and often the right main job, because its intent is the standing state, not any one
piece of work.

### Constituents of the main job

Surveying a domain usually turns up work that seems to **make up** the main job: taking in changes, checking the state
against its sources, putting it right, setting it up in the first place. That is useful discovery, and the topology
records it, as **constituent candidates** on the main job's entry, as input for the job map.

**Constituent candidates are observations, not a decomposition.** Record what discovery turned up and what made it
look like part of the main job; do not go looking for more to complete the list, cover every stage, or account for
everything the job involves. The list is not exhaustive and need not be balanced: two candidates, or none, is fine.
The job map finds the job's structure, and will find work no candidate anticipated.

A constituent candidate is a hypothesis, not a classification. Its work may turn out to be an **episode** of the
main job (an occasion on which the job is done, using some of its map's steps: *take in a change*), a **step** or part
of one (*correct an error found while recording a change*), or a **sub-job** worth a map of its own (*establish the
register*, on formation or after a loss); and these are not exclusive: work covered by the main map may also be worth
mapping on its own, at a finer level. The topology does not settle which; the job map classifies each candidate as it
maps the job. The topology records, for each candidate:

- **what sets it off** (*a change arrives*, *the annual check falls due*, *an error is found*);
- **what was noticed**: the evidence from discovery (it is done when nothing else is under way; it has its own
  completion; it is large and rare; it only happens inside other work);
- **a lean**: *likely an episode*, *likely a step*, *possibly a job of its own*, or *unclear*, formed lightly from the
  tests below. The lean is provisional and does not bind the map; do not call a candidate a job here.

A candidate's **status** is *open* until a map has looked at it, then the map's classification (`job-map`,
*Constituents, and jobs found while mapping*):

- *covered*: its work is represented in the map, with the steps it uses (*covered: Locate to Execute*);
- *separately mappable*: it appears to have a distinct functional objective worth mapping on its own; it may be
  covered as well;
- *outside scope*: its objective belongs to another job, which goes into the jobs table as a related or stakeholder job;
- *unresolved*: still uncertain, or not yet examined, with which and why.

A separately mappable candidate becomes a row in the jobs table, with category `Sub-job`, once it is agreed to be
worth mapping. None of this has to be settled before the map proceeds.

**Name a candidate for its stable objective, not for the failure that triggers it.** *Establish an authoritative
register* (on formation, or after records are lost) is more stable than *Reconstruct the register*, which is named for
one of its triggers; the trigger is then a circumstance of it.

### The five tests

Five tests help judge whether something is a job of its own. **No single test decides**; weigh them together.

| Test | Question | Points to a job when |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent objective** | Is there a meaningful accomplishment distinct from the parent job's objective? | The executor sets out to achieve something they would name and judge on its own (*the vehicle is serviced*), not only a stage towards the parent's result (*the parts are obtained*) |
| **Independent completion** | Can it be completed, and judged successful, in its own right? | It has its own completion condition and its own sense of *done well*, whether or not the parent job's result is reached |
| **Executor** | Is the same functional capacity responsible, or a different one? | A different capacity points to a stakeholder job; the same capacity is compatible with any classification, and decides nothing alone |
| **Context independence** | Does it make sense outside a particular episode of another job? | It can be done, and makes sense, when nothing else is under way; a step only makes sense inside its job's execution |
| **Stable identity** | Would it remain recognisable under different processes and technologies? | It would still be a distinct accomplishment if the tools and procedures changed; one that exists only because of today's mechanism is a step, or a mechanism |

- **A trigger and a completion do not establish a job.** Steps have both.
- **A different success criterion is not a different job.** Checking for consistency and for correctness, or doing
  something quickly and doing it accurately, are different outcomes of the same work unless each is pursued as an
  accomplishment of its own.
- **A shared higher-level objective does not settle it either way.**

**Where the topology uses them.** At the **boundary**, to decide whether a candidate is part of the main job (a
constituent candidate) or a job beside it (related or stakeholder); and lightly, to form a constituent candidate's
**lean**. Whether a constituent is worth mapping on its own is judged by the job map, which weighs the tests in full, by
objective rather than by any sequence of its own.

### Open classifications

Where the topology cannot settle a **boundary** question (part of the main job, or a job beside it; one executor or
two), record it as an **open classification**, with the evidence each way, rather than forcing it. Open is for real
uncertainty that would change the main job's boundary, not a way to avoid deciding; constituent candidates are already
provisional and need not be marked open. A topology with open classifications is complete enough to map from, so long
as the main job and its executor are clear.

## The other jobs

| Category | What it is | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Main job** | The job being analysed: the focal job the map will be built for | Exactly one per topology |
| **Related job** | A distinct job performed by the **same** executor | A **distinct desired outcome**: the executor is after a different result, not just doing something at a different time. Its own trigger, recipient and completion support that, but do not establish it |
| **Stakeholder job** | A job performed by **another** executor that touches the main job | Its executor is not the main executor |

*Related job* keeps Ulwick's meaning: another job the same executor is trying to get done, before, during or after the
main job. *Stakeholder job* is the family's own word for a job of another executor that touches the main job; Ulwick
gives these no single name, and the term should not be presented as his.

For each related or stakeholder job, say **in words how it stands to the main job** (*decides on applications before they
are recorded*; *uses the roll to check eligibility*). Classifying those relationships systematically is job domain
analysis's work; here, a clear phrase is enough. How a related job stands **in time** (*before*, *after*,
*alongside*) may be noted where it helps.

### Which other jobs to include

Many jobs touch any main job; a topology that lists them all stops being useful. **Include material boundaries and
interfaces, not every job that touches the main job.** A related or stakeholder job is included when it marks where
the main job begins or ends, or crosses it in a way that shapes it:

- it **changes** what the main job's object holds, or supplies what the main job must take in (*join the club*);
- it **decides, governs or constrains** what the main job may hold or do (*decide on an application*);
- it **depends on** the main job in a way that shapes what the main job must provide (*establish membership before
  relying on it*, where relying parties need membership on a past date);
- for a related job, it could otherwise be **mistaken for part of the main job** (*provide authoritative information
  about membership*).

Leave out jobs that merely **consume** the main job's result: using the record, reading a report, relying on an
answer, where nothing about the use shapes what the main job must do. Consumers are usually many; include one only
where its need sets a requirement on the main job, and then as one job for every consumer with the same need. Leave
out, too, jobs that touch the main job only through another job already listed.

- **Merge only what is the same job.** Executors are merged into one, with one stakeholder job, only when they pursue the
  same desired outcome, by the same rule as the main executor (*Unify when the desired outcome is the same*). An
  insurer, a competition organiser and a venue manager who each need to confirm someone is a member before relying on
  it pursue one outcome: one stakeholder job (*establish membership before relying on it*), one executor (*relying
  party*). A member resigning, an applicant joining and the executor of a deceased member's estate stand in the same
  relationship to the main job (each changes who is on the roll) but pursue different outcomes: three stakeholder jobs.
  A shared relationship may be shown by grouping their rows together, never by merging their executors or jobs.
- **Record what was left out.** List stakeholder jobs considered and excluded in the main job's notes, with why, so the
  next reader does not add them back.

### Flags for deeper analysis

Some boundaries need more than a topology can give them. Note them in the framing as **flags**, briefly, and leave
them to job domain analysis:

- **Changing the world against recording it.** A job that changes the reality (admitting a member, repairing a vehicle)
  is a different job from one that maintains a representation of it (the roll, the fleet log), usually with a
  different executor, even when one person coordinates both. Flag where the two are entangled, and where **recording
  may itself give the change its effect** (a membership that begins on entry in the roll).
- **Obligations.** Notices, returns and reports owed to others: whether one completes a piece of the main job's work or
  belongs to a separate or broader job.
- **Supplying another job.** Where the main job's record feeds other executors, whether supplying it is a job of the
  main executor's own (a related job, if it pursues its own result) or simply what the stakeholder jobs consume.

### Rules of method are not facts about the domain

The tests in this file decide how to classify what the evidence shows. A classification they produce (this obligation
is a step, these executors are one) is a **proposal about this domain**, held with the evidence behind it, not a
general truth about the domain. Do not write one into the topology as a domain assertion (*each change must be
notified; that notification completes it*) unless the evidence has established both the facts and the boundary. Where
it has not, state the facts as open and the classification as proposed. The same applies to feedback taken from a
review: apply it as a test, not as a conclusion.

### Naming a job from its executor's side

A job is named for what **its executor** is trying to get done, at a breadth that covers its variants. A job named for
an output (*Issue a membership card*, *Produce the report*) usually hides two jobs:

- the **consumer's** job (a member proving they belong, to enter a competition), a stakeholder job with its own executor;
- the **supplier's** job (*provide authoritative information about membership*), which may be a related job of the
  main executor, covering current and historical, routine and disputed requests alike.

Do not conflate them; their outcomes will differ.

### The intent and difference

For every job other than the main job, record its **core intent and how it differs from the main job's**: *what is*
against *what if*, a permanent record against an independent check, a standing state against a transaction. This is
the test that the job is genuinely distinct, and the first place a sub-domain boundary shows: a change of intent, time
horizon, lifecycle, authority or language between two jobs is a boundary signal. Record any as a hypothesis in the
framing; sub-domains are settled through the glossary's folders, not here.

Do not force everything discovered into the main job. The topology exists so that the main job keeps a coherent edge
while the wider domain stays on the page.

## Qualifiers and circumstances

Outcome-Driven Innovation also names **emotional** and **social** jobs. Keep the two apart:

- **emotional**: how the executor wants to feel, or avoid feeling (*avoid the anxiety of being blamed when a member is
  wrongly turned away*);
- **social**: how the executor wants to be perceived by others (*be seen as a dependable keeper of the club's
  records*).

Record them as **qualifiers on the job**, never as jobs of their own; the job map turns them into outcomes on its steps.
Record one only where the evidence supports it. A topology with no qualifiers is fine; a qualifier invented to fill the
slot is not.

**Circumstances.** Situations in which the executor must do materially different things: first-time against routine,
contested against agreed, a large organisation against a small one. Record them on the job. A circumstance that
changes the desired outcome, not just the steps or the scale, is a sign of a different job.

## The jobs left out

Two of Ulwick's categories are deliberately not part of a topology. **Consumption-chain jobs** (acquiring, learning,
using, getting help with a product) and **purchase-decision jobs** (the buyer's financial criteria) are about a
product, not the domain, and belong to product work. A reader arriving from the Ulwick literature should know they were
sent there, not forgotten.

## Framing

A few lines above the jobs:

- the **core executor** (by capacity) and the **main job**, in full;
- **flags** for deeper analysis: seams (where execution leaves any system, and whether recording may give a change its
  effect), obligations, supplying;
- any **sub-domain split** already evident, stated as a hypothesis with the signal that prompted it;
- **boundary questions** left open (the breadth of the main job's object; an obligation that may belong to a broader
  job).

## Ready for mapping

Stop when the **main job, its executor and its principal boundaries** are clear enough to support a job map: the job
statement passes the altitude and breadth tests, the executor is settled, the jobs around the main job that shape its
boundary are placed, and every unresolved classification is recorded as open rather than forced. A small topology is
fine. Do not use the step types to settle the topology: that designs a workflow instead of finding the job.

## Errors to watch for

- **A title as an executor.** *Senior analyst* says where someone sits, not what they are getting done; a title is fine
  as a name only if the capacity is stated.
- **Executors split on scale, or merged by relationship.** A large organisation's version of the job treated as a
  different executor because it is bigger, when its desired outcome is the same; or different executors folded into one
  because they stand to the main job the same way, though their desired outcomes differ: a company paying a dividend
  and a lender taking security both rely on the register, but their jobs differ, so they are not one *relying party*; a
  personal representative and an attorney both act for someone else, but not necessarily towards the same result.
- **A process as a job.** *Process the application form* names today's mechanism. Ask what the form is for.
- **A compound statement.** A job joined with *and*, hiding two jobs or a job and one of its steps.
- **A role's coordination as the role's job.** Work done to help another's job recorded as the executor's own job.
- **A statement that assumes a sequence.** A job worded as following a change that, in some circumstances, it makes.
- **An assurance job with no standard.** *Verify the record* with no word on whether consistency or correctness is
  meant.
- **A job on one test.** A candidate made a job, or kept out of one, on a trigger and completion, a shared higher
  objective, the same executor or a different success criterion alone (consistency and correctness; speed and
  accuracy), instead of weighed on the five tests and, where they split, recorded as open.
- **Constituents as a decomposition.** Candidates sought out to complete a list, one per stage or covering
  everything the job involves, instead of recorded as discovery turned them up.
- **Constituents decided early.** A constituent candidate called a job, or argued into a classification, before the
  job map has examined it; a lean stated as a verdict; a candidate named for the failure that triggers it
  (*Reconstruct the register* where the stable objective is *establish an authoritative register*).
- **Open as a way out.** A boundary question left open where the tests clearly point one way.
- **A topology of everyone.** Related or stakeholder jobs added because they touch the main job or consume its
  result, not because they mark a material boundary or interface; or an org chart wearing a topology: one job per department, executors
  named by title, stakeholder jobs that are the reporting line.
- **A related job on trigger alone.** A job of the main executor separated because it has its own trigger or
  completion, with no distinct desired outcome; or a step promoted to one (*obtain the committee's approval* has no
  life outside the change it approves).
- **A job named for its output.** *Issue a membership card*, hiding the consumer's job and the supplier's.
- **The main job at the wrong altitude or breadth.** Too low and the related jobs are steps of a job nobody has
  stated; too high and the map will have forty steps; too narrow and edge cases need artificial distinctions.
- **A solution in the statement.** *Maintain the roll in the system*: the last three words are the product.
- **A modelling choice stated as a domain rule.** A classification written as though the domain required it.
- **A qualifier forced or mislabelled.** An emotional concern labelled social, or a qualifier nobody expressed.
- **Analysis beyond the need.** A topology grown into a full model of the domain's work before the main job is clear:
  relationships classified systematically (job domain analysis's work), or episodes and steps worked out (the job
  map's).

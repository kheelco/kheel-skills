# Elicitation

## Asking a practitioner

One or two questions at a time, each answer shaping the next. Skip any the evidence already answers.

1. **What are they ultimately trying to get done?** Push up until the answer would still be true in twenty years and
   under a different process. *Process application forms* is not it; *maintain the record of who belongs to the club
   and on what terms* is. Keep how well they want it done (*accurate*, *current*) for its success criteria.
2. **Who is responsible for that?** The responsibility, not a title, a department or a seniority; and whether every
   case in scope has someone formally holding it.
3. **Is it ever finished, or kept in a state?** If kept: **what work is there when nothing new arrives?** (Errors found
   later, disputes, questions about a past date, reconstruction.) Note these, with the work set off by events and
   cycles, as constituent candidates; whether any is a job of its own is for the map.
4. **What edge cases must it cope with?** Cases at the edge of the job's object (for a membership roll: a family
   membership, an honorary member, a suspended member, a move between grades). Do they fit without artificial distinctions?
5. **What must they have got done before this, and what do they turn to after it?** Candidate related jobs, or
   constituents of the main job.
6. **What do they do alongside it that is a different accomplishment?** More candidates, and the ones most often
   mistaken for steps.
7. **Who else has a stake?** Who changes what the job is about, checks it, is answerable for it, uses what it
   produces, governs it, or gets in its way? Candidate stakeholder jobs, each with its own executor. Keep those that mark a
   material boundary or interface, not every one that uses the result, and note the rest as left out.
8. **For each candidate, a few tests.**
   - Is it getting their own job done, or helping someone else get theirs done?
   - Is it part of the main job, or a job beside it? If part of it, record it as a constituent candidate with a lean,
     and leave what it is (episode, step or sub-job) to the map; if the boundary itself is unsettled, record it as open.
   - For candidates to merge: do they pursue the same desired outcome, or only stand to the main job the same way?
   - Does what the candidate is called match what makes it distinct?
9. **Is anything owed to others, or made real by being recorded?** Notices, returns, reports; acts whose recording
   gives a change its effect. Note them as flags for deeper analysis; do not settle them here.
10. **When does the job change?** Circumstances in which the executor must do materially different things, and whether
    the desired outcome changes with them.
11. **How do they want to feel, and how do they want to be seen?** Emotional and social qualifiers, kept apart, and
    only where they say so.
12. **Where does execution leave the room?** Which parts happen in the world (a negotiation, a payment, a court order,
    a signature) and what arrives back from them.

Record each answer's source so the job can be marked attested.

## Harvesting from notes

Given interviews, transcripts or documents, harvest first and ask about the gaps:

- every **verb-object phrase** someone says they have to get done (*we have to keep the roll right*);
- every **"before that we…"** and **"and then it goes to…"**: candidate related jobs, or constituents of the main job;
- every **"when X happens, we…"**, **"every year we…"** and **"if we find a mistake…"**: constituent candidates, with their
  triggers;
- every **other party** named as deciding, transacting, checking, approving, receiving or regulating: candidate stakeholder
  jobs;
- every **"what we're really trying to do is…"**: the core job, usually stated higher than the rest;
- every **output someone asks for** (a certificate, an extract, a confirmation): a supplier's job and a consumer's job,
  to be separated;
- every expression of **anxiety or blame** (emotional) or **reputation** (social): a qualifier, labelled as the speaker
  meant it.

Restate each candidate as verb + glossary noun, from its own executor's side, before triaging it. A candidate that
cannot be stated without a system, document or form in it is a mechanism; ask what it is for.

Everything harvested is at best inferred until a source states it, and a job assembled from passing mentions is the
agent's synthesis. Mark it so, and confirm the main job and executor before anything else.
